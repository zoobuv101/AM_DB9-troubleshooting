Rear Shelf Squeak — A Foam-Tape Fix

2009 DB9 CoupéDone on this car · October 2026← All reference topics

A squeak from the rear shelf, the parcel shelf behind the rear seats (the parts list calls it the package tray), got steadily more annoying on this car and was much worse over the summer heat.

The fix comes from Steve McEvoy's Aston 1936 website and his video on removing the rear centre console. New foam tape goes on the shelf's centre catch. That should let the catch pull the shelf down, and so away from the rear screen, which stops the squeak. Almost all of the work is access: the seats and the centre shelf have to come out to reach the catch.

It's a short, straightforward job. It was done here on a 2009 coupé; the Volante's rear deck is different and isn't covered.

The plan

  1. Remove the rear seat bases and backs
  2. Remove the rear centre console cover
  3. Pull the centre shelf forward to release it from its retaining clip
  4. Unplug the third brake light and lift the shelf out
  5. Clean the parts and treat them with leather conditioner
  6. Fit new foam tape to the centre catch
  7. Refit everything

Step 1 — Remove the rear seats

Take out the rear seat bases and backs. The photo below shows the seat-back catch once the seat is out of the way.

Step 2 — Remove the rear centre console cover

The rear centre console cover comes off next. It exposes the grille screws that, on earlier cars, hold in the subwoofer, and gives access to the two screws that hold in the centre shelf.

This car has no centre subwoofer

On this 2009 car the subwoofer isn't in the centre at all. It's in an enclosure under the rear seat base. Its label reads SUB ENCLOSURE 9G43-18808-GC, AN9XA, 15/09/2008, made in China. The Aston Martin DB9 parts list shows 9G43-18808-GC as the 09MY "Sub woofer & enclosure" (08MY: 8G43-18808-GA).

Steps 3 and 4 — Release the shelf and unplug the third brake light

With the two screws out, pull the shelf forward to release it from the retaining clip towards the back. That gives access to the third brake light plug. Disconnect it, and the centre shelf lifts out.

Step 5 — Clean and treat the parts

While everything was out, all the parts were cleaned and treated with leather conditioner.

Step 6 — Foam tape on the centre catch

This is the fix itself: new foam tape on the centre catch. Following Steve's instructions, the tape should let the catch pull the shelf down, and so away from the rear screen.

Step 7 — Refit

Everything goes back together, with the shelf and the rear seat refitted.

Parts on the shelf, from the DB9 parts list

I used foam tape. For reference, these are the rear shelf parts listed in the Aston Martin dealer parts catalogue (coupé, "Package Tray Assy"):

PartPart numberNote
Package tray assembly4G43-46668-ADWThe tray itself; the diagram shows a round cut-out in it
Panel assembly, package tray4G43-46922-AFWThe trim panel over the tray
Bezel, CHMSL (third brake light)4G43-13B477-ACIts plug is the one to disconnect
Clip, spring4G43-613F60-AADrawn with the third brake light bezel; its exact job isn't confirmed
Foam block (× 2)4G43-46660-AAListed with the tray. Not confirmed as the part behind this squeak

Result

The squeak is fixed. It was done in October, though, and the squeak was a hot-weather one, so the proper test comes next summer.

The same job is in the owner's log, with the photos in order.
