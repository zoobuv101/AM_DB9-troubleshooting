Aston Martin VIN Decoder

DB9 · Vantage · DBS · Virage · Rapide · VanquishFrom Aston's own VIN filings · October 2026← All reference topics

Paste a VIN from a 2005–2018 Gaydon-era Aston Martin (DB9, V8 or V12 Vantage, DBS, Virage, Rapide or the 2012–18 Vanquish). It tells you the model and body, model year, gearbox, the engine's derivative code and power, where the car was built, and whether the check digit is right. The VIN is on the V5C, the plate at the base of the windscreen and the label in the door shut.

Decoded in your browser · your VIN isn't sent anywhere

Why the VIN matters on these cars

The engine number is stamped on the block, and on a V12 in a DB9 you won't see it without taking things apart. The 8th character of the VIN gives the same derivative code (AM04, AM09, AM11 and so on), and that decides which parts, software and settings apply. The 5th character tells you the gearbox, which settles questions such as whether a Vantage has the manual or the Sportshift, or whether a 2017 V12 Vantage S is a dog-leg car.

How the VIN is decoded

Every manufacturer selling cars in the US has to file its VIN scheme with the US safety regulator, NHTSA (under 49 CFR Part 565). These tables come from Aston Martin's own filings, read out of NHTSA's public decoder one character at a time, and checked against real VINs from UK, European and US cars. The power figures in the filing match Aston's published outputs for each engine, which is a good sign the letters mean what they say.

⚠ The US filing has gaps. It misses letters used only on UK and European cars (for example the N400's engine letter G), and it misses two letters used on every 2009 car: C for the 4.7 V8 Vantage and E for the DB9's AM09. Those come from real VINs. Where this page says real VIN, the letter is confirmed from actual cars rather than the filing.

The series letter is the one in the chassis numbers that Aston's service bulletins quote, for example "DB9 up to A12418, V8 Vantage up to C13358, DBS up to E01351".

The check digit

Position 9 is calculated from the other 16 characters using the standard ISO 3779 weighting, so a single wrong character almost always shows up. Every real Aston VIN we've tested has a valid check digit, UK and European cars included, so if the decoder says it doesn't match, look for a typo first.

What it can't tell you

  • History, ownership, finance or theft status. Use an HPI-type check for that.
  • Options and colour. Those are in the build sheet, which the Aston Martin Heritage Trust can supply from the VIN.
  • Special editions (N400, Carbon Black and so on) don't have their own model code; the engine letter sometimes gives them away, as with the N400's G.
  • Cars never sold in the US (One-77, V12 Zagato, Lagonda Taraf, DB10) aren't in the filing.
