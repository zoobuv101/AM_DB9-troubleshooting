ZF 6HP26 Gearbox Service — Fluid, Pan, Seals & Sleeve

2009 DB9 Coupé · Touchtronic IIDone on this car · February 2026← All reference topics

Aston Martin calls the Touchtronic II gearbox "filled for life". ZF, who make the 6HP26, recommend a fluid change from 80,000 km depending on use. This page is a complete DIY service: fluid, the combined pan and filter, the mechatronic seals, the electronics sleeve, a cooler purge and ZF's official level-check procedure. It brings together ZF's service information, the excellent aston1936.com DB9 series, and the service done on this car.

ℹ ZF's own table lists the Aston Martin 6HP26 as 6HP26 X: plastic pan, ZF LifeguardFluid 6, and oil-change Version A. The level check below follows Version A.

Easiest route: a complete kit. This car used the genuine ZF 6HP26 service kit from Gearbox Oils Ltd, which includes the pan/filter, sleeve, valve-body tubes, fill adapter and 10 L of fluid.

Tools

  • — 8 mm and 10 mm sockets, 10 mm hex, T40 Torx, extensions, and a stubby 8 mm hex for the fill plug
  • — 22 mm spanner for the cooler fittings
  • — Low-range torque wrench (8–10 Nm), plus one that reaches 35 Nm
  • — ATF fill pump and adapter, measuring container, large drip tray
  • — Compressed air with a nozzle, and rubber bungs for the cooler lines
  • — Scan tool that reads live data from the gearbox ECU (ThinkDiag, Foxwell NT5xx)
  • — A way to get the car level and high. A QuickJack is ideal.

1 — Access

  1. Lift the car level and as high as safely possible. Remove the rear undertray and the rear subframe crossmember.
  2. Remove the right-hand rear exhaust heat shield (two 10 mm bolts, rear one first) and rotate it down around the pipe to reach the fill plug.

2 — Drain and purge the cooler

  1. Crack the fill plug first (8 mm hex), so you know you can refill. Removing it drains about 2.5 L.
  2. Remove the plastic drain plug (10 mm hex). About 4.5 L drains in total.
  3. Undo the four 22 mm fittings at the thermostatic valve. Unbolt the valve, then drain it.
  4. Blow gentle air into the return line so the fluid comes out of the feed line into a container. Expect about 1.5 L from the cooler circuit. Plug the lines.

3 — Pan off

  1. Loosen all 21 T40 pan bolts by hand, crosswise from the outside in (21 → 1). Leave bolts 1 and 2 in, support the pan, then remove them and lower the pan straight down off the spigot.
  2. Check the spigot O-ring hasn't stayed in its bore, and inspect the magnets for anything chunky.

4 — Electronics sleeve and mechatronic unit

  1. Twist the external connector a quarter-turn anticlockwise and pull it out. Press the nub on the white locking lever (rear right of the mechatronic), pull the lever down, and withdraw the sleeve.
  2. Zip-tie the park override lever upright to disengage Park.
  3. Remove only the 10 T40 mechatronic bolts, not the smaller Torx bolts. Loosen them in sequence 10 → 1 by hand, leaving 1 and 4 until last. Support the unit (it's heavy) and lower it. Another 1–2 L will drain.
  4. Replace the four valve seals (lubricated with ATF, each sitting evenly 1–2 mm proud) and the bridge seal (same orientation as the old one). Don't scratch the machined faces.

5 — Refit

  1. Mechatronic: no solvents, just wipe the faces lint-free. Offer it up with the park fork in its slot. Start bolts 1 and 4 at least 3–4 turns, then finger-start the rest. Using hand tools only, draw it up evenly on 1 and 4 until it seats metal-to-metal, then snug and torque all bolts in sequence. Cut the park-lever zip tie: the cable needs 1–2 mm of free play.
  2. Sleeve: lubricate the O-rings, clean the pins with contact cleaner, and push it home until the lock lever clicks up (don't force the lever). Refit the connector and twist it clockwise to lock.
  3. Pan: clean the mating face, and wet the gasket and spigot O-ring with ATF. Hand-start all 21 bolts (no power tools), then torque to 10 Nm crosswise from the centre outwards.
  4. Thermostatic valve: hand-start the fittings, watching for cross-threading (steel into aluminium). Bracket bolts: 8 mm at 9 Nm, 10 mm at 20 Nm. Fittings to 20 Nm. Refit the drain plug.

6 — Fill and level check (ZF Version A)

⚠ Never start the engine with no fluid in the gearbox. You'll be working under a running car, so chock the wheels, set the parking brake, mind the hot exhaust and ventilate for fumes. Too little fluid damages the gearbox; too much leaks from the overflow when hot and can ignite on the exhaust.
  1. Engine off: pump fluid in through the fill hole until it runs out (about 3 L). Refit the plug.
  2. Turn off the A/C and lights. Start the engine and let the idle settle (ZF: 650–950 rpm). Remove the plug and pump quickly until it dribbles again (about 6 L more). Refit the plug.
  3. Connect the scan tool and read Transmission Oil Temperature from the gearbox ECU (TCM) live data. Start this stage with the fluid below 30 °C.
  4. Engine idling, foot on the brake: select R, D, 2nd and 3rd for about 3 seconds each, then return to P.
  5. Still idling in P, at 30–35 °C: remove the fill plug. If nothing runs out, top up until it does.
  6. Let it run until the fluid reaches 40 °C and the flow has slowed to single drops, then refit the plug. Don't exceed 50 °C. If it's still flowing at 50 °C, let it cool to 30 °C and repeat the check without adding fluid.
  7. Torque the fill plug to 35 Nm, clean up and check for leaks. Refit the heat shield, crossmember and undertray.

At idle the fluid took about 10 minutes to climb from 32 °C to 40 °C (aston1936).

Notes from doing it on this car

  • — With the car at full height on a QuickJack, the whole job is comfortable on your back.
  • — A workshop airline makes purging the remote cooler quick and thorough. Keep the pressure low.
  • — Live temperature from ThinkDiag made the ZF level check easy to follow precisely.
  • — It's worth pausing to look inside with the valve body out: it's a fascinating view of the gearbox.
