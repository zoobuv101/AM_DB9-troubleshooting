Fuel Flap Won't Open — Solenoid & Release Cable

2009 DB9 CoupéDone on this car · August 2024← All reference topics

The fuel filler flap on this car refused to open at the petrol station. The flap is released by a solenoid in the rear wing, working a release catch on a cable, and on this car two separate things had failed. The solenoid is also sold as a fuel flap actuator; this page calls it the solenoid throughout.

First, get fuel in: release it by hand from the boot

The solenoid sits behind the left-hand side wall carpet in the boot, and there's an access hole in the carpet. Through it you can see a white pull ring. Pull the ring down, in the direction the flap opens, and the flap releases manually. That was enough to get the flap open and fuel the car.

⚠ Don't pull the ring too hard. The plastic housing where the cable mounts to the solenoid will break; I found that out the hard way, and I assume the plastic has gone brittle with age. Pulling the ring will get you out of trouble a couple of times, but water getting into the catch end of the cable makes it a ticking time bomb. If the flap is playing up, get the cable replaced.

What had failed

With the solenoid out, its white plastic cable mount had gone brittle with age and broken. I assumed that was the fault and cable-tied it back in position. The solenoid worked and the cable moved, but something still wasn't right.

Following the cable back to the release catch, which is built into the cable, I found the real problem. Water had got into the catch and corroded the spring, which had snapped, so it was working on only about 70% of the spring.

ℹ I considered rebuilding the cable with a new spring, but HWM's price for a replacement was reasonable, so a new cable went in.

Replacing the cable and the solenoid

It took about an hour. The interior side carpet has to come off to get full access to the back of the cable and to the solenoid.

How it went

  1. Remove the interior side carpet in the boot to reach the back of the cable and the solenoid
  2. Work the cable until the flap releases
  3. Rotate the catch body, and remove the cable from the fuel flap end
  4. Insert the new cable through the body inside the filler opening
  5. Rotate the catch body to lock it in place
  6. Connect the cable to the new solenoid body, bolt the new solenoid in and connect the cable plug

The solenoid is a shared part

Research on the solenoid turned up an identical unit used by both BMW and Jaguar. It's a VDO part, and it's the white plastic cable mount that's specific to certain cars, which is how the BMW and Jaguar matches came to light. The Ford-era parts bin strikes again.

The label on this car's solenoid reads 2W93-F9C070-AB, a Jaguar part number, dated 03/03/2009.

The replacement was sold as a Jaguar XJ (X350) 2.7 D 2006 RHD fuel flap actuator motor, 6W93-F9C070-AA (13380594), for £20 on eBay. Similar-bodied VDO units are available, but the important parts are the cable entry and the pull ring, and this one is identical.
